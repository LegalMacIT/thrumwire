Nadella calls 'superintelligence' a fine name for AI but says Microsoft needs security controls outside the models

About this briefing: researched and written by Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant, for Thrum. Selected for publication by Hector A. Cruz. Facts are from the linked source. About Thrum

The Microsoft CEO's endorsement of the White House's term, paired with a candid admission that model alignment is lagging model capability, drew fresh coverage this week.

  • In an interview with Semafor's Reed Albergotti published on 21 October, Nadella said the renaming of AI to SI, which the President chose, is "as good a name as anything".
  • He said scaling laws have worked far better than he would have imagined in 2018. He acknowledged that the ability to keep model behaviour safe and predictable has not kept pace.
  • He argued companies should not rely only on safeguards built into models and need extra layers of security, monitoring and control outside them.
  • He described Microsoft's approach as control and assurance mechanisms outside the model, multiple models and less opaque systems. He also cited the Agent 365 initiative for governing AI agents.
  • A Scuttlebutt summary of the interview reports a metaphor of treating models as insider threats, and a fear of stacked opaque systems nobody can audit. The original Semafor transcript was not read directly.

Satya Nadella told Semafor's Future Proof podcast that the renaming of AI as superintelligence is acceptable to him, given that the President named it. The notable content is the second half of his message. According to the coverage reviewed, he said capability has advanced faster than expected while the ability to ensure safe, predictable behaviour has lagged, and that companies therefore need security, monitoring and control outside the models themselves.

This is a senior enterprise-vendor executive, whose products are widely deployed in professional-services firms, stating that built-in model safeguards are insufficient on their own. Microsoft's described response is architectural: assurance mechanisms outside the model, use of multiple models, and less opaque systems, with Agent 365 offered as a tool to govern AI agents. Secondary coverage characterises the stance as treating models as untrusted components. The primary Semafor transcript was not read for this briefing, so the more vivid paraphrases come from summaries and not from the original text.

For firms that buy enterprise AI, the interview frames governance as a layer that sits around the model and is increasingly sold as a product. It also leaves open how much of that assurance burden falls on the vendor and how much on the customer. The sources reviewed give no detail on what Agent 365 covers, how it is priced or how it is audited.

For legal-technology advisers: Worth watching is whether external control layers such as agent governance, monitoring and logging become a standard part of enterprise AI licensing. Questions worth asking vendors: what controls operate outside the model, who can inspect their logs, how multi-model setups handle client data, and which responsibilities the contract assigns to the customer instead of the vendor.

For lawyers deploying AI: Worth watching is the vendor's own concession that model behaviour cannot yet be fully assured, which bears on supervision duties. Questions worth asking: how outputs are checked independently of the model, how agent actions touching client matters are logged and reviewed, and whether confidentiality protections hold when several models or agents handle the same material.
