SUSE tells 1,900 staff that about 10% of jobs will go, citing automation and AI

About this briefing: researched and written by Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant, for Thrum. Selected for publication by Hector A. Cruz. Facts are from the linked source. About Thrum

An enterprise open source vendor's CEO memo names automation and AI as a reason for a roughly 10% cut. The wording is more explicit than in many recent layoff notices.

  • A memo sent to SUSE's roughly 1,900 employees on Monday 2 November was seen by The Register. In it, CEO Dirk-Peter van Leeuwen announced a global workforce reduction of about 10%.
  • The memo says modern tooling is already changing daily workflows. It says teams can move faster with less operational overhead and flatter reporting lines.
  • The memo did not say which roles are affected. It says management considered where people in those positions can evolve, what they can stop doing and what they need to do more of.
  • The CEO described the cuts as a reflection of shifting organizational needs, not of employees' talent or commitment.
  • The Register notes that SUSE had already said in 2025 that it was doubling down on AI.

SUSE, the privately owned open source enterprise software vendor, told its roughly 1,900 employees on 2 November that about 10% of the global workforce will be cut. The Register reported on the memo from CEO Dirk-Peter van Leeuwen, a former Red Hat executive. The memo calls the decisions painful but necessary. It attributes part of the change to automation and AI-enabled tooling, which it says allows faster work with less operational overhead and flatter reporting lines.

The item stands out for its candour. Many large employers have recently avoided tying cuts directly to AI, and some have explicitly denied the link. Here, the memo links the reduction to AI-related changes in daily workflows. It does not quantify how many roles are affected by AI specifically, and it does not name the functions at risk. Those details were not available in the source reviewed.

For enterprise technology buyers, SUSE supplies Linux and Kubernetes management infrastructure. A vendor restructuring of this size can raise questions about support capacity, roadmap priorities and account coverage. The sources reviewed did not indicate any change to products or support commitments. For professional-services firms, the memo is another data point that AI-enabled efficiency is being used to justify flatter structures and leaner teams, including in technical organisations.

For legal-technology advisers: Worth watching is whether SUSE clarifies which functions (support, engineering, sales) are affected, and whether customer-facing commitments change. Questions worth asking vendors with similar restructurings include how support staffing, SLAs and security-patch response times will be maintained, and whether AI tooling is being used in support or code-maintenance workflows that touch customer environments.

For lawyers deploying AI: This item bears on the firms' infrastructure suppliers more than on lawyers' own tools. Questions worth asking include whether any open source or enterprise Linux platforms underpinning document-management, e-discovery or private AI deployments depend on SUSE support. Also worth asking is how a supplier's reduced headcount might affect incident response and confidentiality-related security patching.

Source: The Register · 2026-11-02 · Global (Germany/Luxembourg-based open source vendor)
