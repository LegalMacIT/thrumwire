Cerebras CEO tells AI leaders their doom warnings are drowning out AI's benefits

About this briefing: researched and written by Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant, for Thrum. Selected for publication by Hector A. Cruz. Facts are from the linked source. About Thrum

A chip-company CEO publicly criticised fellow AI leaders for their risk messaging, with a quip that some should not be allowed to talk in public.

  • Cerebras CEO and co-founder Andrew Feldman spoke at TechCrunch Disrupt 2026.
  • He argued that the AI doomer narrative is not doing the industry any favors.
  • He quipped that some AI leaders 'maybe shouldn't be allowed to talk in public.'
  • He said the promise and potential of AI are being overlooked.

At TechCrunch Disrupt 2026, Cerebras CEO Andrew Feldman pushed back on AI leaders who emphasise catastrophic risk. He argued that the focus on risk is drowning out the benefits. Only the opening portion of the TechCrunch article was reviewed. His specific arguments and any examples beyond that were not available.

The comments are a public split among AI executives. They come as figures such as the IMF's managing director warn about labour-market disruption. A hardware vendor whose revenue depends on AI adoption has an evident commercial interest in a more optimistic framing, and that should be weighed when reading the position.

For professional-services firms, the dispute affects client sentiment. Boards and regulators take their cues from executive rhetoric. Swings between alarm and boosterism affect AI budgets, governance appetite and the demand for risk advice.

For legal-technology advisers: Advise clients not to base AI risk governance on whichever executive narrative is dominant at the moment. Keep risk registers and vendor due diligence tied to concrete use cases. Note that vendors have commercial reasons to downplay risk, and ask for evidence.

For lawyers deploying AI: Treat vendor and executive statements about AI's benefits as marketing until they are tested in the firm's own pilots. Keep verification, confidentiality and supervision controls in place whatever the public mood. Use a measured, documented risk assessment when advising clients caught between the two camps.
